PhonicsQuest – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 September 2026

PhonicsQuest is an educational English-learning app for children. This policy explains, in plain language, what the app does and does not send anywhere, so an adult can decide what to switch on.

The short version

By default, PhonicsQuest works entirely on your own device. There are no user accounts, no sign-in, no advertising, and no analytics tracking of individuals. We do not operate a server that receives your data, and we never receive a learner's work.

There are optional features that do send text off the device — the AI helper and, on some browsers, speech recognition. They are off unless an adult turns them on. Each is described below. If you leave them off, nothing a child writes or says leaves the device.

What is stored, and where

To remember a learner's progress (stars, levels, mastery, and the profile name and avatar you choose), the app saves data on your own device using the browser's local storage (localStorage). This information:

  • is not sent to any server we control — we have no copy of it;
  • stays in the browser you used, and is not synchronised to other devices;
  • can be cleared at any time by clearing the app/browser data.

A profile name is whatever you choose to type — we recommend a first name or nickname, not full names or other identifying details.

Read-aloud recordings

If a child records themselves reading a story, the audio is held in the page's memory for playback and is not uploaded anywhere. It is discarded when the page is closed. Only the resulting words-per-minute figures are saved on the device.

Exporting progress

You can export a learner's progress to a file to move it to another device or give it to a teacher. You choose where that file goes; we never see it. Exported files deliberately exclude any saved AI key and the parent PIN. Treat an export as you would a school report: it contains a child's name and their learning record.

Optional AI features — these do send data to a third party

PhonicsQuest can use an external AI provider to give hints, coach writing and mark some open-ended answers. This is off by default, and works only after an adult enters their own API key for a provider in Settings. Turning it on means:

  • Requests go directly from your device to the provider you chose — currently Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude) or OpenAI. They do not pass through any server of ours.
  • The provider receives the text needed for that feature. For writing coaching and essay feedback, that is the child's full composition draft. For hints and explanations, it is the question, the answer options and the child's answer. For synthesis marking, it is the sentence the child wrote. Some requests also include a short summary of recent practice, such as the stage being worked on.
  • The app does not add the profile name or age to these requests. It cannot, however, remove identifying details a child has written into their own composition — a story about their family, school or address is sent as written. This is worth a word to the child before the feature is used.
  • What the provider then does with that text is governed by their policy and the terms of the account whose key you entered, not by this policy. Please read it before enabling AI for a child, and check whether that account has training-on-your-data switched off.
  • Your API key is stored on your device only, and is excluded from progress exports.

Every AI feature has a working non-AI path. Leaving AI off costs you hints and automated writing feedback; it does not lock any activity.

Speech features

Spoken words and letter sounds are produced by your browser's built-in speech engine. Where the app listens — for example to check a word a child says — it uses the browser's speech-recognition support. On several browsers, including Chrome, that service sends the audio to the browser vendor's servers for processing. That happens under the browser's own privacy policy and is outside our control. The microphone is used only while a listening or recording activity is active, and the browser asks your permission first.

Information we do not collect

  • We operate no server that receives learner data, so no names, emails, phone numbers or addresses are sent to us.
  • No location data.
  • No advertising identifiers, and no behavioural or personalised advertising.
  • No third-party analytics or tracking SDKs.

Children's privacy

PhonicsQuest is intended for children, and is built so that the default configuration collects nothing and sends nothing. We do not claim certification under any particular regime; we have not sought a COPPA, GDPR or PDPA compliance assessment, and this policy should not be read as one.

What we can tell you plainly is where the decisions sit. In the default offline configuration there is no collection of personal data by us. If an adult enables the AI features, text written by the child is sent to the provider they chose, and that transfer is governed by that provider's terms — including whatever those terms say about children's data. If your setting is a school or tuition centre, that choice may need to go through whoever is responsible for data protection there. We encourage parents and teachers to supervise young learners.

Third-party content

The app loads fonts from Google Fonts to display text. This may involve a standard web request to Google's font servers. No PhonicsQuest user data is shared in that request.
